What issuers are actually buying
  • Market acceptance — a credible rating widens the investor pool and satisfies investment-mandate requirements
  • Regulatory capital recognition — many bank and insurance mandates require rated instruments
  • Pricing and placement — rated bonds price tighter and place faster than unrated paper
  • Comparability — standardised opinions across companies, sectors and geographies
Cyclicality — the main risk
  • Transaction revenue rises sharply when companies refinance, spreads tighten, or M&A accelerates
  • It weakens when issuance freezes during market stress or rising rates
  • Q2 2026 IG issuance was up high-20s% — near-peak cyclical conditions
🏰 Moat: Very wide — institutional trust moat + regulatory acceptance + decades of default history data impossible to replicate quickly

Why the S&P 500 cannot be easily replaced
  • Self-reinforcing network effect: more funds track it → more derivatives reference it → more liquidity → higher value to license
  • Its value lies in identity, historical continuity, media recognition, and the global derivative ecosystem built around it
  • Another portfolio of 500 US companies would not carry the same weight — the brand is the standard
  • Coordination problem: all participants would need to switch simultaneously for a competitor index to replace it
Competitors
  • MSCI — closest peer, dominant in non-US equity benchmarks
  • FTSE Russell — strong in UK and global mandates
  • Bloomberg Indices, Nasdaq, Solactive, Morningstar Indexes
🏰 Moat: Very wide — standards-based network effect. Once established, customers face a collective coordination problem to move away.
